My pick

Go with C, and borrow A's three-depth section.

Your plan now names the crypto-native global investor as the primary persona, and C is the only direction that opens with their actual question: what backs this token, and can I get my money out? The lens explains that in one gesture, and nothing else in the category looks like it.

C doesn't show the app itself much. A's sticky verdict → snapshot → reasoning section would slot in after the eight checks and fill that gap. If you'd rather lead with the stock product that works today, A is the safe choice; its hero already cycles through tokens too.

In all three

  • Copy rewritten for stocks and tokenized assets. The live page only covers stocks and says 18 tools; the plan says 19.
  • The NVDA example is consistent now. The live page shows $131.4 against a $142 fair value but says "8% above" it.
  • The waitlist asks two optional questions, what to watch and US or not, so you have the geography data §5 needs for the rail decision. The server keeps them and adds them to the CSV export.
  • Each form tags its source (vault-hero, verdict-foot…), so an A/B test shows which page converts.
  • No third-party scripts, so they fit the current CSP. Reduced-motion support, complete without JavaScript, "Research, not advice" on every page, and a made-up token for the one negative verdict.

To ship one

  1. Copy concepts/<name>.html over public/index.html.
  2. Move <name>.js to public/, point the script tag at /<name>.js, and add that path to PUBLIC_PATHS in server.ts.
  3. Delete the robots tag and the concept-switcher block (both are marked).

Until then the concepts live at /concepts/, behind the invite gate in production.

Numbers on these pages

Every price, yield and verdict is illustrative and labeled as such on the page. Real tokens only get neutral or positive reads; VLLA, the "backing unverified" example, is made up.
