The fine print,
in three bullets.

A 10-K or a token's offering terms come back as three plain-English points, the risks as chips, and one paragraph with Petyr's read.

AAPL · 10-K~120 pages → 3 bullets
  • Services revenue hit an all-time high and is now a quarter of sales, so margins keep improving.
  • iPhone units were flat. Growth now comes from price and the ecosystem, not volume.
  • A new $110B buyback keeps cash flowing back to shareholders.
China exposureRegulatory · DMAHardware cycles

Petyr's readA cash machine that leans more on services each year. The story is durability rather than fast growth.

USDY · Token terms~60 pages → 3 bullets
  • Backed by short-term US Treasuries and bank deposits held by the issuer.
  • Yield builds up in the token's price instead of being paid out.
  • Redeeming directly needs issuer onboarding. Most holders exit by selling on-chain.
US persons excludedIssuer KYC to redeemRate-sensitive

Petyr's readWorks like a tokenized money-market account for people outside the US. Check the eligibility rules before you rely on redemption.
